Abstract

Recent advances in 3D semantic segmentation have significantly improved scene understanding in autonomous driving. However, existing methods are typically developed under a closed-set assumption and evaluated under standard driving conditions, where all categories are known in advance. In real-world driving, autonomous vehicles inevitably encounter previously unseen objects, making closed-set perception insufficient for safe deployment. To address this challenge, we introduce DriveOSS, the first real-world multimodal dataset and benchmark specifically designed for 3D anomaly segmentation and open-set semantic segmentation. It contains 12,661 synchronized and annotated frames captured using six surround-view RGB cameras, a 128-beam LiDAR, and a GPS/IMU unit across 52 real-world driving scenes collected under clear, nighttime, cloudy, rainy, and snowy conditions and is organized into ID (in-distribution) and OOD (out-of-distribution) subsets. The ID scenes contain only known classes and are used for training, whereas the OOD scenes additionally contain unknown objects annotated as the outlier class and are used for validation and testing. Extensive experiments show that unseen objects remain a major challenge for current 3D perception systems.

Key Contributions

🌦️ Multimodal & Adverse Weather

Captured with an Ouster OS1-128 LiDAR (20 Hz), 6 surround-view Basler RGB cameras (10 Hz), and RTK-GPS/IMU (600 Hz) across Clear, Night, Cloudy, Rainy, and Snowy conditions.

🎯 Three Unified 3D Tasks

Jointly benchmarks (1) Closed-Set 3D Semantic Segmentation, (2) 3D Anomaly Segmentation, and (3) 3D Open-Set Semantic Segmentation via 3D mOpen-IoU and Outlier IoU.

🔍 LiDAR & Camera-LiDAR Baselines

Evaluates P3Former, LCPS-lidar, Mask4Former3D, LIDO, LCPS, and Mask4Former3D* with MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, Entropy, and RbA uncertainty scoring.
