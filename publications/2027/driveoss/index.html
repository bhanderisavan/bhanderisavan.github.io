Abstract

Recent advances in 3D semantic segmentation have significantly improved scene understanding in autonomous driving. However, existing methods are typically developed under a closed-set assumption and evaluated under standard driving conditions, where all categories are known in advance. In real-world driving, autonomous vehicles inevitably encounter previously unseen objects, making closed-set perception insufficient for safe deployment. To address this challenge, we introduce DriveOSS, which, to the best of our knowledge, is the first real-world multimodal dataset and benchmark specifically designed for 3D anomaly segmentation and open-set semantic segmentation. It contains 12,661 synchronized and annotated frames captured using six surround-view RGB cameras, a 128-beam LiDAR, and a GPS/IMU unit. The dataset comprises 52 real-world driving scenes collected under clear, nighttime, cloudy, rainy, and snowy conditions and is organized into ID (in-distribution) and OOD (out-of-distribution) subsets. The ID scenes contain only known classes and are used for training, whereas the OOD scenes additionally contain unknown objects annotated as the outlier class and are used for validation and testing. Extensive experiments show that unseen objects remain a major challenge for current 3D perception systems.

How DriveOSS Advances 3D Open-World Perception

Unlike synthetic CARLA benchmarks or clear-weather binary anomaly datasets (e.g., STU), DriveOSS captures physically present real-world road hazards across 5 environmental domains while retaining full 19-class semantic annotations in OOD scenes to jointly evaluate closed-set, anomaly, and open-set segmentation.

Multimodal Sensor Suite & Adverse Weather Coverage

🚗 Hardware Specifications

  • LiDAR (1× Ouster OS1-128): 128 beams, 360° H-FOV, 45° V-FOV (−22.5° to +22.5°), 20 Hz.
  • Cameras (6× Basler ace2R): Surround-view RGB, 1920×1080 resolution, synchronized @ 10 Hz.
  • GNSS/INS (1× Certus RTK): >600 Hz update rate, 0.01 m horizontal & 0.015 m vertical RTK accuracy.
  • Disjoint Splits: 19 ID sequences and 33 OOD sequences recorded on non-overlapping routes.

OOD Hazard Taxonomy & Multi-Stage Annotation QC

📦 1. Containers & Packaging

Cardboard boxes, plastic crates, foam blocks, garbage bags, and small trash bins with diverse surface reflectivities.

🪑 2. Furniture & Fixtures

Office chairs, folding chairs, wooden chairs, clothes racks, and A-frame advertising signs with thin structural parts.

🪜 3. Tools & Industrial Equipment

Ladders, shovels, metal barriers, and maintenance equipment featuring rigid frames and high metallic reflectivity.

🎒 4. Personal Belongings

Backpacks, bicycle helmets, duffle bags, and small low-profile road hazards with irregular geometries.

🛒 5. Utility & Service Equipment

Shopping carts, wheelbarrows, waste-collection carts, and mobile bins encountered in urban zones.

🪵 6. Roadside Debris & Misc.

Wooden pallets, fallen signs, tires, and unstructured debris posing sudden collision risks.

6-Stage Human Refinement & Spatial-Hazard Policy

To eliminate ambiguity between outlier and other-object, any non-ID object on or immediately adjacent to the drivable surface is strictly labeled as outlier, whereas permanent off-road fixtures (e.g., bus shelters, benches) are labeled other-object.

100% of OOD objects are manually annotated from scratch. For known classes, KISS-ICP registered scans are refined via plane removal, class-isolation filtering, and independent dual-annotator verification.
